Foil, Emboss or Letterpress — Which Finish Should You Pick?

These three finishing techniques are the ones we get asked to explain the most, and each creates a completely different feel on the same card. Foil stamping presses a thin layer of metallic or coloured film onto the paper using heat and pressure, giving you crisp, reflective lettering — think shimmering gold names catching the light.

Embossing, by contrast, uses no ink or foil at all. A metal die presses the design into the paper from underneath, raising it into a three-dimensional relief on the surface. It's subtle, tactile, and reads as quiet luxury — guests often run their fingers over an embossed motif before they even read the text.

Letterpress works in the opposite direction: instead of raising the paper, it presses ink deep INTO the paper, leaving a gentle indent you can feel from the back of the card. It has a slightly imperfect, handcrafted character that machine digital printing can't replicate, which is why it's become popular for minimalist, typography-led invitations.

Cost and turnaround also differ. Foil is the fastest and most cost-effective of the three for large quantities. Emboss and letterpress both require custom metal dies made specifically for your design, which adds a few days to production and a one-time die charge — but that die can be reused if you order matching thank-you cards or envelope seals later.

Our general rule of thumb: choose foil when you want maximum shine and colour, emboss when you want understated texture without any colour at all, and letterpress when your design leans heavily on typography and you want that ink-pressed, artisanal look. Many of our most-loved cards actually combine two of the three — foil for the names, emboss for a border motif.
