The Complete Paper & GSM Guide for Invitations

GSM (grams per square metre) is the single most important number when choosing invitation paper, and it's also the one most customers have never heard of before walking into our studio. In simple terms, it tells you how heavy and thick a sheet of paper is — a higher GSM means a sturdier, more premium-feeling card.

For everyday invitations — birthdays, get-togethers, save-the-dates — anything between 250 and 300 GSM works well and keeps printing costs reasonable. For wedding cards, we generally recommend starting at 350 GSM and going up to 600 GSM or more for box invitations and layered panel cards, where the paper itself needs to hold its shape without bending.

Beyond weight, the texture of the paper changes how foil and print sit on the surface. Smooth matte stock gives foil a clean, sharp edge and works beautifully for minimal typographic designs. Textured cotton or handmade paper adds warmth and a tactile, artisanal feel, but foil edges can look slightly softer — which some couples actually prefer for a rustic aesthetic.

Colour of the base paper matters just as much as weight. Pure white paper makes foil colours pop the most vividly, ivory and cream tones feel warmer and more traditional, and dark stock (maroon, navy, black) needs gold or silver foil to be legible, since regular ink print won't show up.

Our recommendation for first-time couples: order a sample kit before finalising anything. Paper photographs very differently from how it feels and looks in hand, and a five-minute touch test usually settles the paper-weight debate faster than any amount of scrolling through reference images.
