Wording Etiquette for Indian Wedding Invitations

Wedding invitation wording in India carries more nuance than a simple date-and-venue announcement — it reflects family structure, religious customs, and the tone you want to set for your celebration. Getting it right matters, and it's one of the most common questions we field before printing even begins.

The order in which families are named is often the first decision point. Traditionally, the bride's family is listed first as the hosts, followed by the groom's family, though many modern couples now list both families together or word the invitation from the couple's own perspective — both are completely acceptable, it's simply a matter of what feels right for your families.

Religious and ceremonial details need to be precise. If you're including a shloka, Quranic verse, or Gurbani line, we always recommend it be reviewed by a family elder or priest before final printing, since even small errors in transliteration can be noticed immediately by guests familiar with the text.

For multi-function weddings — mehendi, haldi, sangeet, wedding, reception — clarity matters more than elaborate language. We suggest a clear hierarchy on the card: main ceremony details in the largest type, followed by a smaller insert card listing all other functions with their own dates, times and venues, rather than cramming everything onto one panel.

Finally, proofread twice, then have someone outside the immediate family proofread a third time. Names, spellings of venues, and dates are the most common last-minute corrections we see, and catching them before printing saves both time and the cost of a reprint.
